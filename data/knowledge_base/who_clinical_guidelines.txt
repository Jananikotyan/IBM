FEVER ASSESSMENT AND MANAGEMENT
==========================================================================

DEFINITION:
Fever is defined as a body temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or above. It is a sign, not a disease.
Most fevers are caused by infections and are part of the body's natural immune response.

CLASSIFICATION BY TEMPERATURE:
- Low-grade fever: 37.5°C to 38.4°C — Monitor at home, rest and fluids
- Moderate fever: 38.5°C to 39.4°C — See a health worker if it persists beyond 2 days
- High fever: 39.5°C to 40.9°C — Seek medical attention promptly
- Very high fever: 41°C or above — EMERGENCY — go to hospital immediately

FEVER IN CHILDREN — RED FLAGS (Seek emergency care immediately):
- Any fever in a baby under 3 months old
- Fever above 40°C in a child under 5 years
- Fever with a stiff neck or headache (may indicate meningitis)
- Fever with a skin rash, especially spots that don't fade when pressed
- Fever with difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
- Fever with seizures (febrile convulsions)
- Fever with extreme drowsiness, not waking up, or unconscious
- Fever with refusal to drink fluids

HOME MANAGEMENT OF MILD FEVER:
- Increase fluid intake — water, ORS, coconut water, diluted juice
- Dress child lightly — do not wrap tightly
- Sponging with lukewarm (not cold) water can help
- Paracetamol may reduce discomfort — dose per body weight, every 6 hours
- Never give aspirin to children — risk of Reye's syndrome
- Monitor temperature every 4–6 hours

WHEN TO SEEK MEDICAL CARE:
- Fever lasting more than 3 days without improvement
- Fever in a child under 1 year lasting more than 24 hours
- Temperature above 39.5°C despite home measures
- Child looks very ill, is not alert, not feeding or drinking

DIARRHOEA AND DEHYDRATION
==========================================================================

DEFINITION:
Diarrhoea is the passage of 3 or more loose or liquid stools per day. Acute diarrhoea
lasts less than 14 days. Persistent diarrhoea lasts 14 days or more. Dysentery is
diarrhoea with blood in the stool.

ORAL REHYDRATION THERAPY (ORT) — WHO STANDARD:
ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) is the first-line treatment for diarrhoea.
Available FREE at all government health centres in India.

WHO ORS Formula (per 1 litre of safe water):
- Sodium chloride (table salt): 2.6 g
- Trisodium citrate: 2.9 g
- Potassium chloride: 1.5 g
- Glucose (anhydrous): 13.5 g
(Or use pre-packaged ORS sachets — dissolve one sachet in 1 litre clean water)

Home ORS Recipe (if ORS packet not available):
- 1 litre clean water (boiled and cooled)
- 6 level teaspoons of sugar
- Half (1/2) level teaspoon of salt
- Add juice of half a lemon for potassium (optional)

HOW MUCH ORS TO GIVE:
- After each loose stool: Under 2 years: 50–100 ml; 2–10 years: 100–200 ml; Older: 200–400 ml
- Continue until diarrhoea stops

SIGNS OF DEHYDRATION — ASSESS CAREFULLY:
Mild: Thirsty, slightly dry mouth — treat at home with ORS
Moderate: Restless or irritable, sunken eyes, dry mouth, skin pinch returns slowly (1–2 sec) — see a doctor
Severe: Lethargic/unconscious, very sunken eyes, unable to drink, skin pinch returns very slowly (>2 sec) — EMERGENCY

CONTINUE FEEDING DURING DIARRHOEA:
- Continue breastfeeding — never stop during diarrhoea
- After ORS, offer food as soon as child is willing to eat
- Extra feeding after recovery to catch up on nutrition

RED FLAGS — SEEK URGENT CARE:
- Blood in stool (dysentery)
- Unable to drink ORS (vomiting everything)
- Signs of severe dehydration
- Diarrhoea lasting more than 14 days
- High fever with diarrhoea
- Passing no urine for 8+ hours

ZINC SUPPLEMENTATION FOR CHILDREN:
WHO recommends zinc supplementation for all children with diarrhoea:
- Children 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg zinc per day for 10–14 days
- Children under 6 months: 10 mg zinc per day for 10–14 days
Zinc reduces duration and severity of diarrhoea and prevents recurrence.
Available free at government health centres.

PREVENTION OF DIARRHOEA:
- Handwashing with soap before eating and after using toilet (most effective intervention)
- Drinking only boiled/treated water
- Safe food preparation and storage
- Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months
- Rotavirus vaccine (available free under India's UIP)

ACUTE RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS (ARI) AND PNEUMONIA
==========================================================================

PNEUMONIA — LEADING KILLER OF CHILDREN UNDER 5:
Pneumonia kills more children than any other infectious disease globally.
In India, it accounts for 14% of all child deaths. It is preventable and treatable.

CLASSIFICATION OF RESPIRATORY ILLNESS IN CHILDREN:
1. Cold / Upper Respiratory Infection: Runny nose, mild cough, no fast breathing — Home care
2. Non-Severe Pneumonia: Fast breathing, no chest indrawing — Oral antibiotics, home care
3. Severe Pneumonia: Chest indrawing, grunting, nasal flaring — Hospital immediately
4. Very Severe Pneumonia: Cyanosis, unable to drink, severely ill — EMERGENCY

FAST BREATHING THRESHOLDS (count breaths for 60 seconds):
- Under 2 months: 60 or more breaths/minute
- 2–11 months: 50 or more breaths/minute
- 1–5 years: 40 or more breaths/minute
- Above 5 years: 30 or more breaths/minute

DANGER SIGNS — GO TO HOSPITAL IMMEDIATELY:
- Chest indrawing (lower chest wall pulls in with each breath)
- Nostrils flare with breathing
- Grunting sound on each breath
- Bluish or grey lips or fingernails (cyanosis)
- Stridor (harsh crowing sound when breathing in)
- Unable to drink or breastfeed
- Convulsions
- Extreme drowsiness, difficult to wake
- Persistent vomiting — cannot keep anything down

HOME CARE FOR MILD COUGH/COLD:
- Keep child comfortable and rested
- Honey (1 teaspoon) can soothe cough in children over 1 year — do NOT give to infants
- Steam inhalation for older children and adults
- Warm fluids help thin mucus
- Do NOT use antibiotics for viral respiratory infections
- Antibiotics are NOT effective against viruses

PNEUMONIA PREVENTION:
- Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV) — free under India's UIP
- Haemophilus influenzae type b vaccine (Hib) — included in Pentavalent vaccine under UIP
- Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months
- Adequate nutrition — malnourished children at higher risk
- Reducing indoor air pollution (cooking fires, tobacco smoke)

MALARIA
==========================================================================

WHAT IS MALARIA:
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites transmitted
by the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Most common in tropical and
subtropical areas including many parts of India.

MALARIA-ENDEMIC STATES IN INDIA:
High burden: Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram,
Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Gujarat tribal areas, Karnataka (forest areas)

SYMPTOMS OF MALARIA:
Classic presentation: Cyclical fever with chills, shivering, sweating, and headache
- Fever — often with teeth chattering and extreme chills
- Profuse sweating when fever breaks
- Severe headache
- Muscle aches and joint pain
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea
- Fatigue and weakness
Symptoms appear 10–15 days after mosquito bite (Plasmodium falciparum)

SEVERE MALARIA — EMERGENCY SIGNS:
- Altered consciousness or coma
- Repeated convulsions
- Difficulty breathing
- Inability to stand or sit without support
- Abnormal bleeding
- Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin)
- Dark or black urine
- Severe anaemia (very pale palms, conjunctiva)
SEVERE MALARIA IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY — Call 108 and go to hospital immediately

MALARIA DIAGNOSIS:
- Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) — available FREE at all PHCs in India
- Microscopy (blood smear) — gold standard
- Any fever with chills in malaria-endemic area: GET TESTED — do not self-medicate

TREATMENT:
- Treatment must be prescribed by a healthcare provider
- Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) is first-line for P. falciparum
- Chloroquine for P. vivax (where not resistant)
- Primaquine for radical cure and prevention of transmission
- FREE treatment at all government health facilities in India
- Complete the FULL course even if you feel better — stopping early causes resistance

MALARIA PREVENTION:
- Sleep under Insecticide-Treated Nets (ITNs) — distributed free by government
- Use indoor residual spraying (IRS) — government spraying programme
- Wear long-sleeved clothing at dusk and dawn
- Eliminate stagnant water around the home
- Apply mosquito repellent on exposed skin

TUBERCULOSIS (TB)
==========================================================================

WHAT IS TB:
Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It primarily
affects the lungs (pulmonary TB) but can affect any organ. TB spreads through
the air when a person with active TB coughs, sneezes, or speaks.

TB IS CURABLE — Complete the full 6-month treatment.
FREE diagnosis and treatment at all government health facilities in India.

SYMPTOMS OF TB:
- Persistent cough lasting 2 weeks or more
- Blood in sputum (coughing up blood)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Night sweats (drenching sweats during sleep)
- Loss of appetite
- Persistent fatigue
- Low-grade fever, especially in evenings
- Chest pain

WHEN TO SUSPECT TB:
Any person with a cough for 2 weeks or more should be tested for TB.
Free testing available at all PHCs, CHCs and district hospitals.

DIAGNOSIS:
- Sputum smear microscopy (free at all government labs)
- GeneXpert / CBNAAT test (free under RNTCP/NTP)
- Chest X-ray
- DSTB (Drug-Sensitive TB) vs DRTB (Drug-Resistant TB) distinction is critical

NATIONAL TB ELIMINATION PROGRAMME (NTP) INDIA:
- Nikshay Portal: All TB patients registered nationally
- Nikshay Poshan Yojana: Rs. 500/month nutritional support for patients on treatment
- FREE drugs: Full DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course) available
- Treatment duration: 6 months for drug-sensitive TB
- DRTB treatment: 9–20 months

TREATMENT — DO NOT STOP EARLY:
Stopping TB treatment before completion leads to Drug-Resistant TB (DRTB),
which is much harder and more expensive to treat and can be fatal.
Complete ALL 6 months even if you feel completely well after 2 months.

PREVENTION:
- BCG vaccine at birth (free under India's UIP) — protects against severe forms in children
- Good ventilation in homes and public spaces
- Cover mouth when coughing (cough hygiene)
- HIV testing in TB patients — HIV-TB co-infection worsens outcomes

ANAEMIA IN WOMEN AND CHILDREN
==========================================================================

DEFINITION:
Anaemia is defined as a haemoglobin (Hb) level below:
- Children 6 months to 5 years: <11 g/dL
- Children 5–12 years: <11.5 g/dL
- Adolescent girls: <12 g/dL
- Pregnant women: <11 g/dL
- Non-pregnant women: <12 g/dL

India has one of the highest anaemia burdens in the world.
Anaemia affects 53% of women of reproductive age and 58% of children under 5 in India.

SYMPTOMS OF ANAEMIA:
- Fatigue and weakness
- Pale skin, pale inner eyelids, pale gums
- Shortness of breath on exertion
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Rapid heartbeat
- Cold hands and feet
- Poor concentration, difficulty studying (in children)

CAUSES IN INDIA:
- Iron deficiency (most common) — poor diet, inadequate iron absorption
- Malaria — destroys red blood cells
- Worm infestation (hookworm) — causes blood loss
- Vitamin B12 and folate deficiency
- Thalassaemia (genetic condition)

TREATMENT AND PREVENTION:
- Iron-Folic Acid (IFA) tablets — FREE at all government health facilities
  * Pregnant women: 1 tablet daily throughout pregnancy + 6 months post-delivery
  * Children 5–10 years: 1 tablet (small dose) weekly under WIFS programme
  * Adolescent girls: 1 weekly tablet under WIFS (Weekly Iron Folic acid Supplementation)
- Dietary iron: Green leafy vegetables, jaggery, beans, lentils, meat, eggs
- Vitamin C increases iron absorption — eat fruits with iron-rich meals
- Avoid tea/coffee with meals — they inhibit iron absorption
- Deworming: Albendazole 400mg once yearly for children 1–5 years (free under NHM)

ANAEMIA IN PREGNANCY:
Severe anaemia in pregnancy is dangerous:
- Risk of premature birth
- Low birth weight baby
- Increased risk of maternal death
All pregnant women should take IFA tablets throughout pregnancy.
Haemoglobin tested at every ANC visit — free at government facilities.

DENGUE FEVER — COMPLETE GUIDE
==========================================================================

WHAT IS DENGUE:
Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection caused by dengue virus (DENV), transmitted by
Aedes aegypti mosquito (also called the tiger mosquito — black and white stripes, bites
during daytime, especially morning and late afternoon).

India records approximately 200,000+ dengue cases annually. Urban and semi-urban areas
are most affected. Rainy season (July–November) is peak dengue season.

SYMPTOMS OF DENGUE — APPEAR 4–10 DAYS AFTER MOSQUITO BITE:
Classic dengue fever:
- Sudden high fever (39–40°C, very characteristic)
- Severe headache (especially behind the eyes — retro-orbital pain)
- Severe muscle and joint pain (called "breakbone fever" for this reason)
- Skin rash (appears 2–5 days after fever onset, red patches on trunk and limbs)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fatigue and weakness
- Loss of appetite

DENGUE SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION:
Dengue without Warning Signs: Fever, nausea, rash, aches — can be managed at home with close monitoring
Dengue with Warning Signs (requires medical attention):
  - Abdominal pain or tenderness
  - Persistent vomiting
  - Clinical fluid accumulation (swelling, difficulty breathing)
  - Mucosal bleeding (nose bleed, gum bleeding)
  - Restlessness or lethargy
  - Liver enlargement >2 cm
  - Rapid decrease in platelet count
Severe Dengue (EMERGENCY — hospitalise immediately):
  - Severe plasma leakage leading to dengue shock syndrome
  - Severe bleeding
  - Severe organ involvement (liver, CNS, heart, kidneys)

WARNING SIGNS — WHEN TO RUSH TO HOSPITAL:
Go immediately to hospital if dengue patient has:
- Bleeding from any site (nose, gums, under skin, in urine or stool)
- Blood-stained vomit or black tarry stool
- Severe abdominal pain
- Rapid breathing or difficulty breathing
- Cold, clammy skin, pale appearance
- Extremely restless or very drowsy
- No urine output for 6+ hours

DENGUE TREATMENT:
There is NO specific antiviral drug for dengue. Treatment is supportive:
- Rest
- Adequate fluid intake — oral rehydration (3 litres/day for adults)
- Paracetamol for fever and pain (NEVER give aspirin or ibuprofen — increases bleeding risk)
- Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, diclofenac, aspirin
- Mosquito nets while patient has fever (to prevent spread to others via mosquitoes)
- Platelet count monitoring — seek medical advice if falling rapidly

DENGUE DIAGNOSIS:
- NS1 Antigen test (detectable in first 5 days of fever)
- Dengue IgM/IgG antibody test
- Complete Blood Count (CBC) — platelet count and haematocrit
Available at government hospitals and private labs

DENGUE PREVENTION:
1. Eliminate mosquito breeding sites (Aedes breeds in clean standing water):
   - Empty, clean or cover all water storage containers weekly
   - Dispose of discarded tyres, pots, tins, coconut shells (hold water)
   - Change water in flower vases and pet water bowls weekly
   - Keep overhead tanks and drums tightly covered
   - Unclog roof gutters so water does not pool

2. Personal protection from mosquito bites:
   - Use mosquito repellent (DEET-based) on exposed skin during daytime
   - Wear full-sleeved clothing, especially in morning and late afternoon
   - Use mosquito nets (especially useful for infants and young children)
   - Use window and door screens
   - Use mosquito coils or vaporisers indoors

3. Government control measures:
   - Source reduction drives by municipal authorities
   - Larval surveillance and larviciding
   - Fogging during outbreaks (adulticiding)
   - Report dengue breeding sites to local municipality

DENGUE VACCINE:
Dengvaxia vaccine exists but is not yet in India's National Immunization Programme.

CHIKUNGUNYA
==========================================================================

WHAT IS CHIKUNGUNYA:
Chikungunya is a viral disease transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes (same as dengue).
Often co-occurs with dengue outbreaks. The name means "to become bent" in
Makonde language, describing the stooped posture from severe joint pain.

SYMPTOMS:
- Sudden onset of high fever
- SEVERE joint pain (distinguishing feature — can last months)
- Muscle pain
- Headache
- Nausea
- Fatigue
- Skin rash

DIFFERENCE FROM DENGUE:
- Joint pain in chikungunya is much more severe and prolonged (can last weeks to months)
- Rarely life-threatening (unlike severe dengue)
- No warning signs that indicate rapid deterioration
- Bleeding complications are rare

TREATMENT: No specific treatment. Rest, fluids, paracetamol for pain and fever.
Prevention: Same mosquito control measures as dengue.

LEPTOSPIROSIS
==========================================================================

WHAT IS LEPTOSPIROSIS:
Leptospirosis is a bacterial disease caused by Leptospira bacteria.
Transmitted through contact with water or soil contaminated by urine of infected animals
(particularly rats). Wading through floodwater is a major risk.

HIGH RISK SITUATIONS:
- Wading or swimming in floodwater
- Agricultural workers (paddy fields)
- Workers in sewers, mines, abattoirs
- After floods — increased rodent activity

SYMPTOMS (appear 2–30 days after exposure):
- High fever
- Severe headache
- Muscle pain (especially calves)
- Chills
- Red eyes (conjunctival suffusion)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Skin rash

SEVERE LEPTOSPIROSIS (Weil's disease — EMERGENCY):
- Jaundice (yellow eyes and skin)
- Kidney failure
- Meningitis
- Lung haemorrhage
- Can be fatal if not treated promptly

TREATMENT: Antibiotics (Doxycycline, Penicillin, Amoxicillin) — prescribed by doctor
PREVENTION:
- Avoid wading in floodwater, especially with cuts or wounds
- Wear rubber boots and gloves in high-risk work
- Do not swim in potentially contaminated water sources
- Doxycycline chemoprophylaxis recommended for high-risk individuals after flood exposure (doctor's prescription needed)

TYPHOID FEVER
==========================================================================

WHAT IS TYPHOID:
Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella typhi bacteria. Spread through contaminated
food or water. Very common in areas with poor sanitation and water quality.

SYMPTOMS (appear 1–3 weeks after exposure):
- Gradually rising fever (rises each day — stepladder pattern)
- Headache
- Weakness and fatigue
- Stomach pain
- Loss of appetite
- Constipation or diarrhoea
- Characteristic: Bradycardia (slow pulse despite high fever)
- Rose spots (faint pink rash on chest/abdomen) in some patients

COMPLICATIONS (if untreated):
- Intestinal perforation (hole in gut) — EMERGENCY requiring surgery
- Internal bleeding
- Encephalopathy (brain involvement)
- Can be fatal if untreated

DIAGNOSIS: Blood culture (gold standard), Widal test (less specific)

TREATMENT:
- Antibiotics (Fluoroquinolones, third-generation cephalosporins, Azithromycin)
- Treatment course is 5–14 days depending on drug chosen
- Drug-resistant typhoid (XDR typhoid) is emerging — report treatment failure to doctor
- Adequate rest and oral fluids

PREVENTION:
- Drink only boiled or treated/bottled water
- Eat thoroughly cooked hot food
- Avoid raw vegetables washed in tap water in endemic areas
- Wash hands thoroughly before eating and after using toilet
- Typhoid Vi polysaccharide vaccine (TCV) — available privately, not yet in NIS

JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS (JE)
==========================================================================

WHAT IS JE:
Japanese Encephalitis is a viral brain infection transmitted by Culex mosquitoes
that breed in rice paddies and stagnant water. Found in rural farming areas.

HIGH-RISK DISTRICTS: About 24 states in India, especially Assam, Uttar Pradesh,
Bihar, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh.

SYMPTOMS:
- High fever
- Headache
- Stiff neck
- Vomiting
- Confusion or altered behaviour
- Can progress to coma and death
- Long-term neurological disabilities in survivors

JE VACCINATION (FREE under UIP in endemic districts):
- JE-1: At 9 months
- JE-2: At 15–18 months
- Given to ALL children in endemic districts

PREVENTION: Mosquito control, avoiding peak biting times (dusk and dawn),
use of insecticide-treated nets.

KALA-AZAR (Visceral Leishmaniasis)
==========================================================================

WHAT IS KALA-AZAR:
Kala-azar (meaning "black fever") is a parasitic disease transmitted by the
bite of sandflies (Phlebotomus). It is fatal if untreated.

ENDEMIC AREAS IN INDIA: Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh.

SYMPTOMS (develop weeks to months after sandfly bite):
- Prolonged irregular fever (weeks to months)
- Significant weight loss
- Enlarged spleen (splenomegaly) — main clinical feature
- Enlarged liver
- Swollen lymph nodes
- Anaemia (very pale appearance)
- Darkening of the skin (especially on face, hands — hence "black fever")
- Progressive weakness

DIAGNOSIS: rK39 rapid test, blood smear, PCR — available free at PHCs in endemic areas.

TREATMENT: Free treatment at all government health centres in endemic areas.
India has a National Kala-Azar Elimination Programme — target to eliminate by 2023.
Treatment: Liposomal Amphotericin B (single-dose IV injection under NVBDCP)

PREVENTION:
- Indoor residual spraying (IRS) — government programme
- Use insecticide-treated bed nets
- Apply mosquito repellent
- Report fever lasting more than 2 weeks in endemic areas to PHC immediately

CHOLERA
==========================================================================

WHAT IS CHOLERA:
Cholera is an acute intestinal infection caused by Vibrio cholerae bacteria.
Spread through contaminated water or food. Can cause rapid, severe dehydration and death.

SYMPTOMS:
- Sudden onset of profuse, watery "rice-water" diarrhoea (very characteristic)
- No pain, no blood (unlike dysentery)
- Rapid dehydration
- Muscle cramps
- Vomiting
- Can progress to shock and death within hours if untreated

CHOLERA IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY:
- A person with suspected cholera can lose 10–20 litres of fluid per day
- Death can occur within hours without treatment
- Immediately start ORS and get to hospital

TREATMENT:
- Aggressive oral rehydration with ORS
- IV fluids for severe cases (at hospital)
- Antibiotics shorten duration — prescribed by doctor
- With prompt treatment, mortality is less than 1%

PREVENTION:
- Drink only boiled or treated water — MOST IMPORTANT
- Wash hands with soap after toilet use and before eating
- Cook food thoroughly, eat while hot
- Avoid raw shellfish and raw vegetables in outbreak areas

RABIES
==========================================================================

WHAT IS RABIES:
Rabies is a viral disease that affects the brain and nervous system. Almost always fatal
once symptoms appear. Caused by rabies virus, transmitted through saliva of infected animals.

IN INDIA: Dogs are responsible for 97% of rabies deaths. 
India accounts for about 36% of world's rabies deaths.

TRANSMISSION: Bite, scratch, or lick on broken skin/mucous membranes of infected animal.

POST-EXPOSURE PROPHYLAXIS (PEP) — URGENT:
If bitten or scratched by an animal:
1. IMMEDIATELY wash wound with soap and water for 15 minutes — most important first step
2. Apply betadine (povidone iodine) or alcohol
3. Go to hospital/PHC IMMEDIATELY — do not wait for symptoms
4. Get anti-rabies vaccination series (4 doses: Day 0, 3, 7, 14) — FREE at government hospitals
5. Get rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) for severe bites (Category III) — FREE at government hospitals

WOUND CATEGORIES:
Category I: Touching/feeding animal, lick on intact skin — No PEP needed
Category II: Nibbling, superficial scratches without bleeding — Wound washing + vaccine
Category III: Single or multiple transdermal bites, contamination of mucous membrane with saliva — Wound washing + vaccine + RIG (URGENT)

PREVENTION:
- Vaccinate pet dogs and cats
- Avoid contact with stray dogs, cats, monkeys, bats
- Do not provoke animals
- Anti-rabies PEP is available FREE at all government hospitals — act fast

IMPORTANT: Once rabies symptoms appear, there is NO cure. PEP MUST be started BEFORE symptoms.

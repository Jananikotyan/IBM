PREVENTIVE HEALTHCARE — BASICS
==========================================================================

HANDWASHING — SINGLE MOST EFFECTIVE PREVENTION:
Proper handwashing prevents: Diarrhoea (40% reduction), Respiratory infections (20% reduction),
Typhoid, Cholera, Hepatitis A, Worm infestations

CRITICAL MOMENTS for handwashing:
1. Before eating or preparing food
2. After using the toilet or defecating
3. After cleaning a child who has defecated
4. After handling animal waste
5. After caring for a sick person

CORRECT HANDWASHING TECHNIQUE (WHO 6-step method):
1. Wet hands with clean water
2. Apply soap — cover all surfaces
3. Rub palms together
4. Rub back of each hand with palm of other hand
5. Rub between fingers, interlaced
6. Rub backs of fingers
7. Rub each thumb in a rotational manner
8. Rinse with clean running water
9. Dry with clean cloth or air-dry
Duration: At least 20 seconds of scrubbing

SAFE WATER AND SANITATION:
- Boil water for at least 1 minute to kill all pathogens (3 minutes at high altitude)
- Chlorination: Add 2 drops bleach (5% chlorine) per litre of water, wait 30 minutes
- Water purification tablets: Follow package instructions
- Use a clean, covered container to store drinking water
- Draw water with a clean ladle — do not dip hands into storage vessel
- Install and use a toilet — open defecation spreads diseases

SAFE FOOD PRACTICES:
- Cook food thoroughly — especially meat, eggs, seafood
- Eat cooked food while hot
- Store food in clean, covered containers
- Refrigerate perishables within 2 hours
- Use separate cutting boards for raw meat and vegetables
- Wash all fruits and vegetables before eating
- Avoid reheating food more than once

NON-COMMUNICABLE DISEASES (NCDs)
==========================================================================

HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
Definition: Blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg on two separate measurements
India has an estimated 188–225 million hypertension patients (many undiagnosed).

Normal blood pressure: Less than 120/80 mmHg
Elevated: 120–129 / less than 80 mmHg — lifestyle changes
Stage 1 hypertension: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg — lifestyle changes + monitor
Stage 2 hypertension: ≥140 / ≥90 mmHg — medication needed

SYMPTOMS: Hypertension is mostly SILENT — no symptoms until serious complications.
When symptoms occur: Headache (back of head), dizziness, vision changes, nosebleeds.

COMPLICATIONS OF UNCONTROLLED HYPERTENSION:
- Heart attack (myocardial infarction)
- Stroke (brain attack)
- Kidney failure
- Heart failure
- Blindness

RISK FACTORS:
- Excess salt in diet (India: average intake is double WHO recommended 5 g/day)
- Obesity and overweight
- Physical inactivity
- Tobacco use
- Alcohol consumption
- Stress
- Family history
- Diabetes

PREVENTION AND MANAGEMENT:
- Reduce salt intake: Don't add extra salt, avoid processed/packaged foods
- Eat fruits and vegetables daily (minimum 400 g per day — WHO recommendation)
- Regular physical activity: At least 150 minutes moderate activity per week
- Maintain healthy weight
- Quit tobacco in all forms
- Limit alcohol
- Check blood pressure regularly — FREE at all PHCs under NHM
- Take prescribed medications regularly — do NOT stop when you "feel better"

HYPERTENSION SCREENING: FREE at all government PHCs and Wellness Centres under
National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, CVD (NPCDCS)

DIABETES MELLITUS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
India is the "Diabetes Capital of the World" — 80+ million diabetics.

TYPES:
Type 1: Immune system destroys insulin-producing cells — usually in young people
Type 2: Most common — body develops insulin resistance — usually in adults
Gestational Diabetes: Develops during pregnancy — increases risk of complications

SYMPTOMS (Type 2 — often no symptoms until advanced):
- Increased thirst (polydipsia)
- Frequent urination, especially at night (polyuria)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Extreme hunger despite eating
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing cuts and wounds
- Recurring infections (skin, urinary, gum)
- Numbness or tingling in hands and feet

DIAGNOSIS:
- Fasting blood sugar: ≥126 mg/dL on 2 separate occasions = Diabetes
- Random blood sugar: ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms = Diabetes
- HbA1c: ≥6.5% = Diabetes (reflects average blood sugar over 3 months)
- Prediabetes: Fasting 100–125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7–6.4%

FREE DIABETES SCREENING at all PHCs under NPCDCS programme.

MANAGEMENT:
- Diet: Low sugar, low refined carbohydrates, high fibre, regular meals
- Physical activity: 30 minutes walking per day significantly lowers blood sugar
- Blood sugar monitoring: Regular HbA1c testing
- Medications: Oral hypoglycaemics (Metformin is first-line) or insulin — prescribed by doctor
- Eye exam: Annual for retinopathy (diabetes-related blindness)
- Foot care: Daily inspection, never walk barefoot, proper footwear
- Blood pressure control: Critical in diabetics — target <130/80

DIABETES EMERGENCY — HYPOGLYCAEMIA (LOW BLOOD SUGAR):
Symptoms: Shaking, sweating, confusion, fast heartbeat, hunger, pale skin
Treatment: Immediately give 15–20 g fast-acting sugar (3–4 glucose tablets, or half cup juice)
Severe hypoglycaemia (unconscious): Call 108 IMMEDIATELY — do NOT give anything by mouth

CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE (HEART DISEASE AND STROKE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
India accounts for approximately 20% of global CVD deaths.
CVD is the leading cause of death in India.

HEART ATTACK SYMPTOMS — CALL 108 IMMEDIATELY:
- Chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or tightness
- Pain radiating to left arm, jaw, neck, back, or stomach
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat
- Nausea and vomiting
- Lightheadedness or sudden dizziness
NOTE: Women may have atypical symptoms — fatigue, nausea, back pain without chest pain.

STROKE SYMPTOMS — CALL 108 IMMEDIATELY (FAST test):
F — Face drooping: One side of face droops or is numb. Ask person to smile.
A — Arm weakness: One arm is weak or numb. Ask to raise both arms.
S — Speech difficulty: Slurred, strange, or unable to speak.
T — Time to call 108: If ANY of these are present — call immediately.
Every minute counts in a stroke — "Time is Brain."

PREVENTION:
- Control hypertension and diabetes — most important risk factors in India
- Quit tobacco completely (smoking doubles heart disease risk)
- Eat a heart-healthy diet: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, fish
- Reduce saturated fats, trans fats, sugar
- Regular physical activity: 150 minutes/week
- Limit alcohol
- Manage stress (yoga, meditation, social support)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE (COPD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Major cause of disability and death in India — often underdiagnosed.

CAUSES IN INDIA:
- Tobacco smoking (most important)
- Indoor air pollution from burning biomass fuel (wood, cow dung, crop residue) — affects rural women
- Outdoor air pollution
- Occupational dust and chemicals

SYMPTOMS:
- Progressive shortness of breath (breathlessness on exertion)
- Chronic cough with mucus/phlegm
- Wheezing
- Frequent respiratory infections

PREVENTION:
- Never start smoking; quit if you do (benefits begin within hours of quitting)
- Improve indoor ventilation — cook outdoors or install a chimney/smokeless stove
- Use LPG or cleaner cooking fuels — Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana provides free LPG connections for BPL families
- Wear a mask in dusty environments

GOVERNMENT HEALTH SCHEMES — KNOW YOUR RIGHTS
==========================================================================

AYUSHMAN BHARAT PRADHAN MANTRI JAN AROGYA YOJANA (PM-JAY):
India's largest health insurance scheme.
- Coverage: Up to Rs. 5 lakh per family per year for hospitalisation
- Beneficiaries: Approximately 50 crore (500 million) people (bottom 40% of population)
- Eligibility: Based on SECC 2011 data + state government lists
- Cashless treatment at all empanelled government AND private hospitals
- Covers 1,579+ treatment packages including surgeries, chemotherapy, ICU
- Check eligibility: Call 14555 (toll-free) or visit pmjay.gov.in

HOW TO AVAIL PM-JAY:
- Get Ayushman Bharat card (Ayushman card) from PHC, Common Service Centre, or online
- Show card at any empanelled hospital for cashless treatment
- No premium, no prior registration, no deductible

NATIONAL HEALTH MISSION (NHM) — FREE SERVICES AT PHCs:
Under NHM, ALL of the following are FREE at government health facilities:
- Outpatient consultations
- Diagnostic tests (blood, urine, X-ray at PHC level)
- Medicines from the essential drug list
- Ambulance services (108, 102)
- Maternal and child health services
- Immunization
- Family planning services
- Management of common diseases

RASHTRIYA SWASTHYA BIMA YOJANA (RSBY):
Health insurance for BPL workers in the unorganised sector.
Coverage: Rs. 30,000 per year for hospitalisation.
Merged into PM-JAY in most states.

PRADHAN MANTRI UJJWALA YOJANA (PMUY):
Free LPG connection for BPL households.
Prevents indoor air pollution from biomass cooking fuel.
Benefits: Reduces respiratory disease, reduces burns, reduces time spent collecting fuel.
Apply at: Nearest LPG distributor, online at pmuy.gov.in

NIKSHAY POSHAN YOJANA (FOR TB PATIENTS):
Rs. 500 per month nutritional support for all TB patients on treatment.
Available to all TB patients registered in Nikshay portal.
Paid directly to patient's bank account via DBT.

MENTAL HEALTH
==========================================================================

MENTAL HEALTH IS HEALTH — Seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
India has a large mental health treatment gap — most people with mental illness receive no care.

COMMON MENTAL HEALTH CONDITIONS:

DEPRESSION:
Symptoms: Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, sleep changes, feeling worthless,
          difficulty concentrating, thoughts of death or suicide
Duration: Symptoms present for more than 2 weeks

ANXIETY:
Symptoms: Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, poor sleep, difficulty concentrating,
          irritability, physical symptoms (fast heartbeat, sweating, shortness of breath)

WARNING SIGNS — SEEK URGENT HELP:
- Talking about wanting to die or kill oneself
- Looking for ways to kill oneself
- Talking about being a burden to others
- Giving away prized possessions
- Sudden calmness after period of depression (may indicate decision to end life)

SUICIDE PREVENTION — ACT IMMEDIATELY:
iCALL (TISS) Helpline: 9152987821 (Mon–Sat, 8am–10pm)
Vandrevala Foundation: 1860-2662-345 (24/7)
Snehi: 044-24640050

National Mental Health Programme (NMHP):
Free mental health services at district hospitals and Mental Health Institutes.
ASHA workers can refer for psychiatric care.
Medicines for depression, schizophrenia, epilepsy — free at government hospitals.

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY AND MENTAL HEALTH:
Regular physical activity (30 minutes, 5 days a week) reduces depression and anxiety
as effectively as antidepressants in mild-moderate cases.

ORAL HEALTH
==========================================================================

COMMON ORAL PROBLEMS IN INDIA:
- Dental caries (tooth decay) — very common, preventable
- Periodontal disease (gum disease) — leading cause of tooth loss
- Oral cancer — India has one of the highest rates in the world (due to tobacco use)
- Oral submucous fibrosis — from areca nut (pan masala, supari) use

PREVENTION:
- Brush teeth twice daily (morning and before bed) with fluoride toothpaste
- Floss daily
- Reduce sugar and sugary drinks
- AVOID tobacco in all forms (smoking, smokeless tobacco, gutka, pan masala, supari)
- Avoid excess alcohol
- Visit a dentist for check-up every 6 months (free at dental colleges)

ORAL CANCER WARNING SIGNS — SEE A DOCTOR:
- White or red patches in the mouth that don't heal
- A sore in the mouth lasting more than 3 weeks
- Difficulty opening the mouth (submucous fibrosis — from areca nut use)
- Lump or thickening in mouth, throat
- Difficulty swallowing
- Numbness of tongue

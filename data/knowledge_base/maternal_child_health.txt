ALL ANTENATAL AND MATERNAL HEALTH SERVICES ARE FREE AT GOVERNMENT HEALTH FACILITIES

==========================================================================
ANTENATAL CARE (ANC) — CARE DURING PREGNANCY
==========================================================================

WHY ANC MATTERS:
Proper care during pregnancy reduces risk of complications, ensures healthy birth,
and prevents maternal and newborn deaths. India has made significant progress but
maternal mortality remains a concern in rural areas.

WHEN TO REGISTER FOR ANC:
Register as early as possible — ideally BEFORE 12 weeks of pregnancy.
Register at: Your nearest PHC, CHC, Sub-centre, or district hospital.
Get your Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card — keep it safe.

MINIMUM ANC VISITS (WHO recommends 8 contacts):
1st Visit: Before 12 weeks — Register, baseline tests, confirm pregnancy, start IFA tablets
2nd Visit: 14–26 weeks — Blood pressure, weight, fundal height, foetal heartbeat
3rd Visit: 28–34 weeks — Anaemia check, foetal position, birth preparedness counselling
4th Visit: 36 weeks onwards — Final assessment, birth plan, review warning signs

ESSENTIAL ANC SERVICES (all FREE at government facilities):
1. Blood pressure monitoring (every visit) — detect pre-eclampsia/eclampsia
2. Weight measurement (every visit) — monitor maternal nutritional status
3. Haemoglobin test (every trimester) — detect anaemia
4. Blood group and Rh factor (at registration)
5. Blood sugar test (screen for gestational diabetes)
6. HIV testing (at registration) — free, confidential under PPTCT programme
7. Urine test (protein and sugar) — detect kidney issues, gestational diabetes
8. Syphilis test (VDRL) — free at all government facilities
9. Abdominal examination — foetal growth, position, heart rate
10. Ultrasound — free at district hospitals and empanelled private facilities

IRON-FOLIC ACID (IFA) SUPPLEMENTATION — MUST TAKE THROUGHOUT PREGNANCY:
- 1 IFA tablet daily from the time of registration throughout pregnancy
- Continue for 3 months after delivery (postpartum)
- Take with water — NOT with tea, coffee, or milk (reduces absorption)
- May cause dark stools and slight nausea — normal
- If nausea is severe, take at night before bed
- IFA tablets available FREE at PHC, sub-centre, ASHA worker

WHY IFA IS CRITICAL:
Iron prevents anaemia — leading cause of maternal death in India.
Folic acid prevents neural tube defects in the baby (spina bifida, anencephaly).

CALCIUM SUPPLEMENTATION:
- 500 mg calcium twice daily from 4 months onwards
- Prevents hypertension and pre-eclampsia
- Reduces risk of preterm birth
- Free at government health facilities

TT/Td VACCINATION IN PREGNANCY:
- TT-1: As early as possible in pregnancy
- TT-2: 4 weeks after TT-1
- Protects mother and baby against tetanus

DANGER SIGNS IN PREGNANCY — SEEK EMERGENCY CARE IMMEDIATELY:
- Heavy vaginal bleeding at any stage
- Severe abdominal pain
- Severe headache not relieved by rest
- Blurred vision or visual disturbances
- Swelling of face, hands, feet (sudden onset)
- Convulsions/seizures (eclampsia — life-threatening)
- Decreased baby movement after 28 weeks
- Fever above 38°C
- Water breaking before labour is due (premature rupture of membranes)
- Baby not moving for 12 hours (after 28 weeks)

CALL 108 (ambulance) for any danger sign above.

INSTITUTIONAL DELIVERY — GIVE BIRTH AT A HEALTH FACILITY
==========================================================================

WHY DELIVER AT A HEALTH FACILITY:
- Most maternal and newborn deaths occur around the time of birth
- Skilled birth attendants can manage complications immediately
- Emergency care (caesarean section) available at FRUs and district hospitals
- COMPLETELY FREE at government hospitals under Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)

JANANI SURAKSHA YOJANA (JSY):
Cash incentive scheme to promote institutional delivery:
- Rural women: Rs. 1,400 cash benefit after delivering at a government facility
- Urban women: Rs. 1,000 cash benefit after delivering at a government facility
- BPL (Below Poverty Line) families: Additional benefits
- Plus ASHA worker receives incentive for accompanying the mother

JANANI SHISHU SURAKSHA KARYAKRAM (JSSK):
Free entitlements for pregnant women and sick newborns at government facilities:
- FREE delivery (including caesarean)
- FREE drugs and consumables
- FREE diagnostic tests
- FREE blood (if required)
- FREE diet during stay (3 days for normal delivery, 7 days for C-section)
- FREE transport from home to facility and back
- FREE treatment for newborns for 30 days

PRADHAN MANTRI MATRU VANDANA YOJANA (PMMVY):
- Rs. 5,000 cash benefit for 1st living child
- Given in 3 instalments: Rs. 1,000 at ANC registration, Rs. 2,000 after 6 months, Rs. 2,000 after delivery
- For all pregnant women including government employees (first child benefit)

==========================================================================
POSTNATAL CARE (PNC) — CARE AFTER DELIVERY
==========================================================================

CRITICAL PERIOD: The first 24 hours after delivery — most deaths occur here.

ROUTINE POSTNATAL VISITS:
- 1st visit: Within 24 hours of delivery
- 2nd visit: On day 3
- 3rd visit: On day 7 (especially for home deliveries)
- 4th visit: At 42 days (6 weeks postpartum)

DANGER SIGNS AFTER DELIVERY — SEEK CARE IMMEDIATELY:
- Heavy bleeding (more than 2–3 pads per hour)
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
- High fever
- Severe headache, blurred vision, convulsions
- Inability to urinate
- Swollen, red, painful legs
- Breast pain with fever (mastitis)
- Extreme sadness, confusion (postpartum depression/psychosis)

BREASTFEEDING GUIDANCE:
- Initiate breastfeeding within 1 HOUR of birth — very important
- Colostrum (first yellow milk) — give, do NOT discard. It is rich in antibodies and nutrients.
- Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months — no water, no other food or milk
- Breastfeed on demand — 8–12 times per day for newborns
- Correct latch is essential — baby's mouth covers areola, not just nipple
- Continue breastfeeding until 2 years alongside complementary foods
- Breastfeeding protects against: diarrhoea, pneumonia, malnutrition, SIDS

NEWBORN CARE
==========================================================================

IMMEDIATE NEWBORN CARE (at delivery):
1. Dry the baby immediately — prevents heat loss (hypothermia kills newborns)
2. Keep baby warm — skin-to-skin contact with mother (kangaroo care)
3. Initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour
4. Delayed cord clamping — at least 1–3 minutes after birth
5. Apply chlorhexidine to cord stump — prevents infection (omphalitis)
6. Eye care — 1% tetracycline eye ointment to prevent neonatal conjunctivitis
7. Vitamin K injection — prevents haemorrhagic disease of the newborn

BIRTH WEIGHT AND MEASUREMENT:
- Normal birth weight: 2,500 g or more
- Low birth weight (LBW): Less than 2,500 g — needs special care
- Very low birth weight: Less than 1,500 g — needs NICU care
Weigh baby within 1 hour of birth. Babies lose 5–7% weight in first week (normal).

KANGAROO MOTHER CARE (KMC) — FOR LOW BIRTH WEIGHT BABIES:
- Place baby skin-to-skin on mother's chest, between breasts
- Cover both with a cloth/shawl
- Baby should be in upright position, slightly tilted
- Continuous KMC for at least 8 hours/day
- Helps regulate temperature, promotes weight gain, reduces infection risk
- Especially important for premature and low birth weight babies

DANGER SIGNS IN NEWBORNS — SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
- Not breathing or gasping at birth (call for help immediately)
- Blue or grey colour (cyanosis)
- Convulsions/seizures
- Fever (temperature above 37.5°C) or hypothermia (<36.5°C)
- Rapid breathing (>60 breaths per minute)
- Grunting with every breath
- Chest indrawing
- Bulging fontanelle (soft spot on head)
- Jaundice appearing within 24 hours of birth, or severe jaundice
- Not feeding — not sucking or swallowing
- Redness spreading from umbilicus (naval) — omphalitis
- Bleeding from any site
- Lethargy, not responsive

NEWBORN JAUNDICE:
- Physiological jaundice (normal): Appears Day 2–3, peaks Day 4–5, resolves by Day 10–14
- Phototherapy (blue light) treatment — free at government hospitals
- Pathological jaundice (danger): Appears before 24 hours, very severe, or lasts >2 weeks — EMERGENCY

CHILD GROWTH AND NUTRITION
==========================================================================

COMPLEMENTARY FEEDING (from 6 months):
Continue breastfeeding AND start solid foods at 6 months.
- Start with soft, mashed, semi-solid foods
- Frequency: 2–3 times per day at 6–8 months, 3–4 times at 9–23 months
- Gradually increase variety: cereals, pulses, vegetables, fruits, dairy, eggs, meat
- Add 1 extra meal per day during and after illness (catch-up feeding)

GROWTH MONITORING:
- Weigh baby monthly at Anganwadi Centre for first 2 years
- Growth chart (weight-for-age): Plotted in MCP card / growth register
- Grade 1 malnutrition (MAM — Moderate Acute Malnutrition): MUAC 12.5–11.5 cm
- Grade 2 malnutrition (SAM — Severe Acute Malnutrition): MUAC <11.5 cm — needs hospital treatment
- MUAC: Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (measure with tape at mid-point of upper arm)

SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION (SAM):
SAM in children is a medical emergency. Signs:
- Very low weight-for-height (Z-score < -3)
- MUAC less than 11.5 cm
- Bilateral pitting oedema (both feet swell when pressed)
FREE treatment at:
- NRC (Nutrition Rehabilitation Centres) at district hospitals
- Community-based management with RUTF (Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food)
- Therapeutic feeding: F-75 and F-100 milk formulas

VITAMIN A SUPPLEMENTATION:
Critical for: Preventing blindness, boosting immunity, reducing child deaths
Schedule: Every 6 months from 9 months to 5 years — given at PHC/Anganwadi
Free under National UIP programme

FAMILY PLANNING — FREE SERVICES
==========================================================================

All family planning services are FREE at government health facilities.

SPACING METHODS:
- Copper-T (IUCD 380A): Inserted at PHC, effective for 10 years
- Oral Contraceptive Pills (OCPs): Mala-N / Mala-D — free at sub-centres
- Condoms: Free at sub-centres, PHCs, ASHA workers
- Injectable contraceptive (Antara — DMPA): Quarterly injection, free at PHC
- Centchroman (Chhaya): Weekly/fortnightly pill — non-hormonal, free

PERMANENT METHODS (Sterilisation):
- Female sterilisation (tubectomy): Free at district hospitals (compensation given)
- Male sterilisation (vasectomy): Free, simpler procedure (higher compensation given)
- Compensation under Mission Parivar Vikas: Rs. 2,000–3,000 for tubectomy; Rs. 3,000–4,000 for vasectomy

EMERGENCY CONTRACEPTION:
- Emergency Contraceptive Pill (ECP): "I-pill" or "Unwanted-72"
- Take within 72 hours of unprotected intercourse (the sooner the better)
- Available at PHCs, ASHA workers
- Does NOT cause abortion — prevents fertilisation

COUNSELLING:
ASHA workers and ANMs provide family planning counselling.
Ask at your nearest PHC or Anganwadi for more information.

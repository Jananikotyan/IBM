India Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) — National Immunization Schedule
Source: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India — NIS 2024
ALL VACCINES UNDER UIP ARE FREE AT GOVERNMENT HEALTH FACILITIES

==========================================================================
NATIONAL IMMUNIZATION SCHEDULE (NIS) — COMPLETE SCHEDULE
==========================================================================

ALL children and pregnant women are entitled to FREE vaccination at:
- Sub-centres (nearest government health post)
- Primary Health Centres (PHC)
- Community Health Centres (CHC)
- Anganwadi Centres
- District Hospitals
- Vaccination days: Tuesdays and Fridays at most PHCs

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT BIRTH (given in hospital/delivery centre)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin)
   - Protects against: Tuberculosis (TB), especially severe forms in children
   - Dose: 0.05 ml intradermal, left upper arm
   - Given: Within 24 hours of birth, before hospital discharge
   - If missed at birth: Give as soon as possible, up to 1 year of age

2. OPV-0 (Oral Polio Vaccine — Zero Dose)
   - Protects against: Poliomyelitis
   - Dose: 2 drops oral
   - Given: Within 24 hours of birth
   - Note: This is a "birth dose" — separate from the primary series

3. Hepatitis B — Birth Dose
   - Protects against: Hepatitis B virus infection (can cause liver cirrhosis and cancer)
   - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular, right thigh
   - Given: Within 24 hours of birth (ideally within 1 hour of birth)
   - Critical: Early dose prevents mother-to-child transmission of Hepatitis B

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 6 WEEKS (1.5 months)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OPV-1 (Oral Polio Vaccine — 1st dose)
   - Dose: 2 drops oral

5. Pentavalent-1 (DPT + Hep B + Hib combined vaccine)
   - Protects against: Diphtheria, Pertussis (whooping cough), Tetanus, Hepatitis B, H. influenzae type b (Hib)
   - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular, left thigh
   - Note: Pentavalent replaces separate DPT + Hep B + Hib injections

6. Rotavirus Vaccine-1 (RVV-1)
   - Protects against: Rotavirus diarrhoea (leading cause of severe diarrhoea in children)
   - Dose: 5 drops oral
   - Note: Given in select states/districts — check with your PHC if available

7. fIPV-1 (Fractional dose Inactivated Polio Vaccine)
   - Dose: 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm
   - Protects against: Polio (injectable form for broader immunity)

8. PCV-1 (Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine)
   - Protects against: Pneumonia, meningitis, and other pneumococcal diseases
   - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular
   - Note: Available in selected states under phased roll-out

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 10 WEEKS (2.5 months)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. OPV-2
   - Dose: 2 drops oral

10. Pentavalent-2
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular, left thigh

11. Rotavirus Vaccine-2 (where available)
    - Dose: 5 drops oral

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 14 WEEKS (3.5 months)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. OPV-3
    - Dose: 2 drops oral

13. Pentavalent-3
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular, left thigh

14. Rotavirus Vaccine-3 (where available)
    - Dose: 5 drops oral

15. fIPV-2
    - Dose: 0.1 ml intradermal, right upper arm

16. PCV-2 (where available)
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 9 MONTHS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
17. MR-1 (Measles-Rubella — 1st dose)
    - Protects against: Measles and Rubella (German measles)
    - Dose: 0.5 ml subcutaneous, right upper arm
    - Measles is highly infectious and can cause blindness, brain damage, death
    - Rubella in pregnancy causes congenital rubella syndrome in babies

18. Vitamin A — 1st dose
    - Dose: 1 lakh (100,000) IU oral (blue capsule)
    - Prevents: Night blindness, reduces child mortality, boosts immunity
    - Given along with MR-1

19. JE-1 — Japanese Encephalitis Vaccine (1st dose)
    - Given only in JE-endemic districts (24 states)
    - Dose: 0.5 ml subcutaneous
    - Protects against: Japanese encephalitis (mosquito-borne brain infection)

20. PCV Booster (where available)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 15–18 MONTHS (Boosters)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
21. MR-2 (Measles-Rubella — 2nd dose/booster)
    - Dose: 0.5 ml subcutaneous
    - Second dose ensures over 95% protection

22. DPT Booster-1
    - Booster for Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular
    - Given at 15–18 months (separate from Pentavalent series)

23. OPV Booster
    - Dose: 2 drops oral

24. Vitamin A — 2nd dose
    - Dose: 2 lakh (200,000) IU oral (red capsule)

25. JE-2 (2nd dose in endemic districts)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VITAMIN A SUPPLEMENTATION SCHEDULE (all children)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 9 months: 1 lakh IU
- 15 months: 2 lakh IU
- 21 months: 2 lakh IU
- Every 6 months until 5 years: 2 lakh IU
Total doses: 9 doses between 9 months and 5 years
Purpose: Prevents blindness, reduces severity of infections, reduces child mortality by up to 25%

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 5 YEARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
26. DPT Booster-2
    - Booster for Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 10 YEARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
27. Td (Tetanus-diphtheria)
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VACCINES AT 15–16 YEARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
28. Td Booster
    - Final Tetanus-diphtheria booster
    - Dose: 0.5 ml intramuscular

PREGNANT WOMEN — VACCINATION SCHEDULE
==========================================================================

All vaccines for pregnant women are FREE at government health facilities.

TT/Td VACCINATION IN PREGNANCY:
- TT-1: Give as early as possible in pregnancy (first contact with health system)
- TT-2: Give 4 weeks after TT-1
- TT Booster: If the woman received 2 TT doses in a previous pregnancy within the last 3 years
              — give 1 booster dose only

PURPOSE: Protects mother and baby against tetanus (Neonatal Tetanus is life-threatening)

Td vaccine is now recommended over TT in many states — both protect against tetanus.

IMPORTANT FOR PREGNANT WOMEN:
- Get vaccinated as EARLY as possible in pregnancy
- Do not delay vaccination due to first trimester concerns — TT/Td is safe throughout pregnancy
- Record the vaccination date in the Mother-Child Protection (MCP) card

PULSE POLIO IMMUNIZATION (National Polio Immunisation Days)
==========================================================================
- Given 2 drops oral to ALL children under 5 on National Immunization Day (NID)
- Held on Sundays in January/February each year
- ADDITIONAL to routine OPV schedule — does not replace it
- India was declared POLIO FREE in 2014 — must maintain high coverage

==========================================================================
IMPORTANT INFORMATION ABOUT VACCINATION
==========================================================================

AFTER VACCINATION — WHAT IS NORMAL:
- Mild fever for 1–2 days — give paracetamol, increase fluids
- Redness, slight swelling, tenderness at injection site — normal, goes away in 2–3 days
- BCG produces a small lump that ulcerates and heals over 6–8 weeks — THIS IS NORMAL
- Baby may be fussy and cry more — this is normal for 1–2 days

WHEN TO SEEK CARE AFTER VACCINATION:
- High fever (above 39.5°C) persisting beyond 2 days
- Excessive swelling at injection site (more than 3 cm)
- Child seems very unwell, unusually drowsy, stops feeding
- Convulsions after vaccination (rare — report immediately)

CONTRAINDICATIONS (reasons NOT to vaccinate on that day):
- Severe illness on the day of vaccination — reschedule, do not delay vaccination permanently
- Severe allergic reaction to a previous dose of the same vaccine
- Live vaccines (OPV, MR) should be postponed if child on immune-suppressing medicines

MYTH BUSTING:
- Myth: "Vaccines cause the disease they protect against" — FALSE (vaccines use killed or weakened germs)
- Myth: "Too many vaccines weaken the immune system" — FALSE (children handle millions of germs daily)
- Myth: "Natural infection gives better immunity" — FALSE (infection risks serious complications)
- Myth: "If my child is healthy they don't need vaccines" — FALSE (vaccines prevent the infection from occurring)
- Myth: "Vaccines contain harmful preservatives" — The quantities are minuscule and proven safe

MISSED VACCINES — CATCH-UP GUIDANCE:
- It is NEVER too late to vaccinate
- Missed vaccines should be given as soon as possible
- You do NOT need to restart the schedule if vaccines are delayed
- Ask your ASHA worker, ANM or PHC about catch-up vaccination

THE MCP (MOTHER-CHILD PROTECTION) CARD:
- Keep the MCP card safe — it records all vaccinations for your child
- Bring it to every vaccination session
- It is your child's vaccination passport
- Lost MCP card: Get a new one from your PHC/ASHA worker

VILLAGE-LEVEL IMMUNIZATION RESOURCES
==========================================================================

WHO TO CONTACT FOR VACCINATION:
1. ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) worker — your nearest village health worker
   - Will inform you of upcoming vaccination days
   - Can accompany you to the vaccination session
   - Provides health education about vaccines

2. ANM (Auxiliary Nurse Midwife) — conducts vaccination at sub-centre level
   - Responsible for vaccination in 3–5 villages
   - Holds regular vaccination sessions at the sub-centre

3. Anganwadi worker — vaccination sessions also held at Anganwadi centres

4. PHC (Primary Health Centre) — fixed vaccination every Tuesday and Friday
   - Provides all vaccines in the National Immunization Schedule free of charge

IMMUNIZATION HELPLINE: 1800-11-1565 (Toll free, available 9am–6pm)
